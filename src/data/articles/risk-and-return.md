# Risk and Return

In investing, **risk** is the chance that an investment loses value or doesn't grow as much as you hoped. **Return** is what you earn: price growth plus any income like dividends or interest.

## The basic trade-off

Investments with higher potential returns usually come with higher risk:

- **Lower risk, lower return:** savings accounts, U.S. Treasury bills.
- **Medium:** bonds and bond funds.
- **Higher risk, higher potential return:** stocks and stock funds.

No investment offers high returns with no risk. Anyone promising that is a red flag.

## Kinds of risk

- **Market risk:** prices fall across the market.
- **Inflation risk:** your returns don't keep up with rising prices.
- **Interest rate risk:** bond prices fall when interest rates rise.
- **Liquidity risk:** you can't sell quickly without taking a loss.

## Managing risk

You can't avoid risk entirely, but you can manage it: spread money across different investments (diversification), match your investments to your time horizon, and only take as much risk as you can live with.
