# Growth vs. Value Stocks

Investors often sort stocks into two broad styles: growth and value.

## Growth stocks

Companies expected to grow their sales and profits faster than average.

- Often reinvest profits instead of paying dividends.
- Prices can be high relative to current earnings, because investors are paying for expected future growth.
- Can rise quickly, but can also fall sharply if growth disappoints.

## Value stocks

Companies whose shares look inexpensive compared with measures like their earnings or assets.

- Often more established, slower-growing businesses.
- More likely to pay dividends.
- Investors hope the market will eventually recognize their worth.

## Which is better?

Neither style wins all the time. Growth stocks have led in some periods and value stocks in others. Many investors simply own both, for example through a broad index fund that holds the whole market.

## Keep in mind

These labels describe a style, not a guarantee. A "value" stock can stay cheap for a long time, and a "growth" stock can stop growing.
