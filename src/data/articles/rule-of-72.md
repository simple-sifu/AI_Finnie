# The Rule of 72

The Rule of 72 is a quick way to estimate how long it takes money to double with compound growth.

## How it works

Divide 72 by the yearly growth rate:

- At 6% a year: 72 ÷ 6 = about **12 years** to double.
- At 8% a year: 72 ÷ 8 = about **9 years**.
- At 2% a year: 72 ÷ 2 = about **36 years**.

## Why it's useful

It makes the power of growth easy to picture. At 8%, $10,000 could become about $20,000 in 9 years, $40,000 in 18 years and $80,000 in 27 years.

It also works for costs. If prices rise 3% a year, they roughly double in 24 years, which shows how inflation shrinks what your money can buy.

## Keep in mind

It's an estimate, most accurate for rates between about 4% and 12%. Real returns change from year to year, so actual doubling times vary. For exact numbers, use a compound interest calculator.
