# Bond Credit Ratings

A credit rating is a grade that tells you how likely a bond issuer is to pay its interest and return your money. Independent rating agencies assign these grades.

## Investment grade vs. high yield

- **Investment grade:** higher ratings (for example, BBB or better on one common scale). Issuers are considered more likely to pay.
- **High yield (sometimes called "junk"):** lower ratings. These bonds pay more interest to make up for a higher chance of default.

## The trade-off

Lower-rated bonds pay higher interest because you're taking more risk. If the company runs into trouble, it might miss payments or fail to repay you.

## Ratings can change

A rating isn't permanent. If a company's finances get worse, its rating can be cut, and its bond prices usually fall.

## U.S. Treasuries

Bonds issued by the U.S. government are generally considered among the safest in terms of credit risk, which is why they often pay less interest than corporate bonds.
