# Why Bond Prices Fall When Interest Rates Rise

Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions. When rates go up, existing bond prices go down, and the other way around.

## Why it happens

Say you own a bond paying 3% interest. Then rates rise, and new bonds pay 5%. Nobody will pay full price for your 3% bond when they can buy a new one paying 5%. To sell yours, you'd have to accept a lower price.

If rates fall to 2% instead, your 3% bond looks attractive, and its price rises.

## Longer bonds move more

The longer a bond's time to maturity, the more its price reacts to rate changes. A 30-year bond's price swings much more than a 2-year bond's.

## Does this matter if I hold to maturity?

If you hold an individual bond until it matures and the issuer pays, you get your full face value back regardless of price swings along the way. Bond funds don't mature, though, so their value moves with rates.

## The upside of rising rates

When rates rise, new bonds and bond funds start earning higher interest. Over time, that higher income can make up for the price drop.
