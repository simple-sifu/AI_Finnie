# Paying Off High-Interest Debt First

Before investing extra money, it often makes sense to pay off high-interest debt like credit cards.

## Why

Paying off a credit card charging 22% interest is like earning a guaranteed 22% return. Investments rarely offer returns that high, and never guaranteed.

## A simple comparison

| Choice | Likely result |
|---|---|
| Put $1,000 toward a 22% credit card | Save about $220 a year in interest, guaranteed |
| Invest $1,000 at an average of 7% | Earn about $70 a year, not guaranteed |

## What about lower-interest debt?

For lower-rate debt, like many mortgages or some student loans, the choice is less clear. Many people invest while paying those down on schedule.

## A balanced approach

1. Keep a small emergency fund so new surprises don't go on the card.
2. Contribute enough to get your full employer 401(k) match.
3. Put extra money toward the highest-interest debt first.
4. Once that's gone, redirect those payments to investing.

## Keep in mind

Always make at least the minimum payment on every debt to avoid fees and damage to your credit.
