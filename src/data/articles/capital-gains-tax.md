# Capital Gains Taxes

A capital gain is the profit you make when you sell an investment for more than you paid. In a regular (taxable) brokerage account, capital gains are taxed when you sell.

## Short-term vs. long-term

- **Short-term:** you held the investment **one year or less**. Taxed at your regular income tax rate.
- **Long-term:** you held it **more than one year**. Taxed at lower rates: 0%, 15% or 20%, depending on your income.

Holding investments for more than a year can noticeably cut your tax bill.

## Capital losses

If you sell at a loss, you can use that loss to offset gains. If losses are bigger than gains, you can deduct up to $3,000 a year ($1,500 if married filing separately) against other income, and carry the rest forward to future years.

## Retirement accounts are different

Buying and selling inside a 401(k), IRA or HSA doesn't create capital gains tax. Tax rules for those accounts apply when you withdraw instead.

## Dividends

Many dividends from U.S. stocks ("qualified dividends") are taxed at the same lower long-term rates.
