# Corporate and High-Yield Bonds

Corporate bonds are loans to companies. Companies issue them to raise money for things like expansion, equipment or refinancing debt.

## Interest and risk

Corporate bonds usually pay more interest than U.S. Treasuries because there's a greater chance the company won't pay. How much more depends on the company's credit rating.

- **Investment-grade corporate bonds:** from financially strong companies; moderate interest.
- **High-yield bonds:** from companies with weaker finances; higher interest and a higher chance of default.

## What happens if a company fails

If a company goes bankrupt, bondholders are paid before stockholders, but they may still get back only part of their money, or none.

## How beginners usually own them

Most beginners own corporate bonds through bond funds or ETFs, which spread risk across many issuers instead of depending on one company.
