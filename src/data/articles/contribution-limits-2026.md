# 2026 Retirement Contribution Limits

The IRS sets yearly limits on how much you can put into retirement accounts. These are the limits for **2026**. They usually change every year, so check the IRS for the current year.

## 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457 plans and the Thrift Savings Plan

- **Employee contribution limit:** $24,500
- **Catch-up contribution, age 50 and older:** an extra $8,000 (total $32,500)
- **Higher catch-up, ages 60 to 63:** an extra $11,250 instead of $8,000

## IRAs (traditional and Roth combined)

- **Contribution limit:** $7,500
- **Catch-up contribution, age 50 and older:** an extra $1,100

## Roth IRA income phase-out ranges

- **Single and head of household:** $153,000 to $168,000
- **Married filing jointly:** $242,000 to $252,000

## Traditional IRA deduction phase-out (if covered by a workplace plan)

- **Single:** $81,000 to $91,000
- **Married filing jointly (contributing spouse covered):** $129,000 to $149,000

## Keep in mind

Employer matching contributions don't count toward your $24,500 employee limit; they fall under a separate, higher overall limit.
