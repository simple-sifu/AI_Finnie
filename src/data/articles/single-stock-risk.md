# The Risk of Owning Just a Few Stocks

Owning just one or a few stocks, or having a large share of your money in one company, is called **concentration risk**.

## Why it's risky

A single company can run into trouble for reasons that have nothing to do with the overall market: a failed product, a scandal, new competition or bad management. If a big part of your money is in that company, its bad news becomes your bad news.

## A common situation

Many people end up concentrated without meaning to: buying a stock after a tip, or owning lots of their employer's stock. If you work for the company too, both your paycheck and your savings depend on the same business.

## How diversification helps

Spreading your money across many companies means one company's problems have a much smaller effect on you. A broad index fund can hold hundreds or thousands of companies in one investment.

## A rule of thumb

Many guides suggest keeping any single stock to a small slice of your portfolio. Finnie flags any individual stock above 20% of your portfolio as a concentration risk.
