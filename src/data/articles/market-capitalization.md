# Market Capitalization

Market capitalization (or "market cap") is the total value of a company's shares. It's calculated by multiplying the share price by the number of shares.

**Example:** a company with 500 million shares at $40 each has a market cap of $20 billion.

## Size categories

Companies are often grouped by market cap. The exact cutoffs vary, but a common rough guide is:

- **Large-cap:** over about $10 billion. Well-known, established companies.
- **Mid-cap:** about $2 billion to $10 billion.
- **Small-cap:** under about $2 billion.

## Why it matters

- **Large-cap** stocks tend to be more stable, with smaller price swings.
- **Small-cap** stocks can grow faster but tend to be more volatile.

## Market cap and indexes

Many indexes, including the S&P 500, weight companies by market cap. That means the biggest companies have the biggest effect on the index's moves.

## A common mistake

A low share price doesn't mean a company is small or cheap. A $5 stock can have a bigger market cap than a $500 stock if it has many more shares.
