# How Your Trade Gets Executed

When you click "buy" in a brokerage app, your order goes on a short journey before it's filled.

## Where your order can go

Your broker decides where to send your order to be filled. Options include:

- a stock **exchange**,
- a **market maker**, a firm that stands ready to buy and sell,
- another trading venue.

## Bid, ask and spread

- **Bid:** the highest price a buyer is currently willing to pay.
- **Ask:** the lowest price a seller is currently willing to accept.
- **Spread:** the gap between them. Heavily traded stocks usually have tiny spreads.

With a market order, you generally buy near the ask and sell near the bid.

## Best execution

Brokers have a duty to seek the best execution reasonably available for your order. That isn't always the single best price, since speed and likelihood of filling also matter.

## Settlement

After a trade, ownership and money officially change hands a short time later. This is called settlement. Most U.S. stock trades settle one business day after the trade.
