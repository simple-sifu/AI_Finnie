# Expense Ratios

An expense ratio is the yearly fee a mutual fund or ETF charges, shown as a percentage of your investment. It covers the fund's management and operating costs.

## How it's charged

You don't get a bill. The fee is taken out of the fund's assets, which slightly lowers your returns.

**Example:** with a 0.50% expense ratio, you pay about $50 a year for every $10,000 invested.

## Typical ranges

- **Broad index funds:** often under 0.10%.
- **Actively managed funds:** often 0.5% to 1% or more.

## Why it matters so much

Fees come out every year, whether the fund does well or not, and they compound over time. A difference that looks tiny, like 0.05% versus 0.75%, can add up to thousands of dollars over decades.

## How Finnie uses it

When you upload your portfolio, Finnie calculates your **weighted average expense ratio**: the overall fee rate across your funds, based on how much you have in each. Individual stocks don't have expense ratios.
