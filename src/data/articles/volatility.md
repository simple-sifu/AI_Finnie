# Volatility

Volatility describes how much and how quickly an investment's price moves up and down.

## High vs. low volatility

- **High volatility:** big, frequent price swings. Individual stocks, especially smaller or newer companies, often behave this way.
- **Low volatility:** smaller, steadier moves. Short-term bonds and money market funds are examples.

## Volatility isn't the same as losing money

A volatile investment can drop sharply and then recover. You only lock in a loss if you sell while it's down. That's why time horizon matters: the longer you can wait, the less short-term volatility should matter to you.

## Why it still matters

- If you need your money soon, a sudden drop could force you to sell at a loss.
- Big swings can be stressful and tempt people into emotional decisions.

## Reducing volatility

- Diversify across many investments.
- Hold some bonds alongside stocks.
- Keep money you'll need soon in steadier investments or savings.
