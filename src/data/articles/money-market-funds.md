# Money Market Funds

A money market fund is a type of mutual fund that invests in very short-term, high-quality debt, like Treasury bills and short-term loans to companies and banks.

## What they're used for

- Holding cash in a brokerage account.
- Saving for short-term goals.
- A lower-risk place to park money between investments.

## How they work

Most money market funds aim to keep a stable value of $1.00 per share and pay interest that moves with short-term interest rates.

## Money market fund vs. money market account

These sound alike but are different:

- A **money market account** at a bank is a deposit account, typically insured by the FDIC up to legal limits.
- A **money market fund** is an investment. It is **not** FDIC insured, and while losses are rare, it can lose value.

## Keep in mind

Money market funds are designed for safety and easy access, not growth. Over the long run, their returns may not keep up with inflation.
