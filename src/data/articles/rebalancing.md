# Rebalancing

Rebalancing means adjusting your portfolio to bring it back to your target mix of investments.

## Why your mix drifts

Say your target is 60% stocks and 40% bonds. If stocks have a great year, you might end up at 70% stocks and 30% bonds. Now you're taking more risk than you planned.

## How to rebalance

- **Sell some of what grew** and buy more of what fell behind, or
- **Direct new contributions** to the part that's below target (no selling needed).

## How often

Common approaches:

- **On a schedule:** once or twice a year.
- **By threshold:** when any part drifts more than about 5 percentage points from its target.

## Keep in mind

- In a taxable account, selling can trigger capital gains taxes. Rebalancing inside retirement accounts avoids this.
- Rebalancing can feel backwards, since you're selling what's doing well. That's the point: it keeps your risk where you chose it.
- Target date funds rebalance automatically.
