# Saving vs. Investing

Saving and investing both set money aside for the future, but they do different jobs.

## Saving

- **Where:** bank savings accounts, money market accounts, certificates of deposit (CDs).
- **Good for:** emergencies and goals in the next few years, like a car or a vacation.
- **Risk:** very low. Bank deposits are typically insured by the FDIC up to legal limits.
- **Growth:** slow. Interest may not keep up with inflation.

## Investing

- **Where:** stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs.
- **Good for:** goals that are many years away, like retirement.
- **Risk:** higher. Values go up and down, and you can lose money.
- **Growth:** higher potential over the long run.

## Using both

Most people need both. A common approach is to save first for an emergency fund, then invest for long-term goals. The closer you are to needing the money, the more of it usually belongs in savings rather than investments.
