# Employer Matching Contributions

An employer match is money your employer adds to your 401(k) or similar plan when you contribute.

## How it works

Matches are usually a percentage of what you put in, up to a portion of your salary. A common formula:

> 50% match on contributions up to 6% of salary.

If you earn $50,000 and contribute 6% ($3,000), your employer adds $1,500. If you contribute only 3% ($1,500), they add just $750.

## Why it matters

A match is an instant return on your money before any investment growth. Not contributing enough to get the full match means leaving part of your pay on the table.

## Vesting

Some employers make you work a certain number of years before the matched money is fully yours. This is called **vesting**. Money you contribute yourself is always 100% yours.

## Where to find your details

Check your plan documents or ask HR for:

- the match formula,
- the vesting schedule,
- whether the match goes into traditional or Roth funds.
