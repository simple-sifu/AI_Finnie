# Interest Rates and the Federal Reserve

The Federal Reserve ("the Fed") is the central bank of the United States. One of its main jobs is setting monetary policy, which influences interest rates across the economy.

## The Fed's goals

The Fed aims for **maximum employment** and **stable prices**. It targets inflation of 2% over the longer run.

## How it works

The Fed's Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meets regularly and sets a target for the **federal funds rate**, the rate banks charge each other for overnight loans. That rate affects many others, including those on credit cards, mortgages, savings accounts and bonds.

## Why it matters to investors

- **Higher rates:** borrowing costs more, savings accounts pay more, existing bond prices tend to fall, and stocks can come under pressure.
- **Lower rates:** borrowing is cheaper, savings pay less, existing bond prices tend to rise, and stocks may benefit.

## In the news

Markets often move on Fed announcements and even hints about future rate changes. For long-term investors, it's useful to understand these moves without overreacting to them.
