# Building an Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected expenses, like a car repair, a medical bill or a job loss.

## Why it comes first

Without one, a surprise expense can push you into high-interest debt or force you to sell investments at a bad time. An emergency fund protects both your budget and your long-term plans.

## How much to save

A common guideline is **three to six months of essential expenses**: rent, food, utilities, insurance and minimum debt payments. If that feels out of reach, start smaller. Even $500 can cover many common emergencies.

## Where to keep it

Somewhere **safe and easy to reach**, such as a savings account at an FDIC-insured bank. It shouldn't be invested in stocks, which could be down right when you need the money.

## How to build it

- Set up an automatic transfer on payday, even a small amount.
- Put windfalls, like a tax refund, toward it.
- Track your progress toward a specific target.

## After you use it

Refill it as soon as you can, so it's ready next time.
