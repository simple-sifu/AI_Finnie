# Net Asset Value (NAV)

Net asset value (NAV) is the price of one share of a mutual fund. It's calculated once each trading day.

## How it's calculated

NAV = (value of everything the fund owns − what it owes) ÷ number of shares.

**Example:** a fund holds $100 million in investments, owes $1 million and has 5 million shares. Its NAV is ($100M − $1M) ÷ 5M = **$19.80 per share**.

## When it matters

- Mutual fund orders are filled at the NAV calculated after the market closes that day.
- ETFs also have a NAV, but you buy and sell them at the market price during the day, which is usually very close to the NAV.

## A common misunderstanding

A low NAV doesn't make a fund "cheap," and a high NAV doesn't make it expensive. A $10 fund isn't a better deal than a $100 fund; what matters is how the fund's investments perform and what it costs.
