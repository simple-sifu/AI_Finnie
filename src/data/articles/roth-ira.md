# Roth IRAs

A Roth IRA is a retirement account where you contribute money you've already paid tax on, and qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free.

## How it differs from a traditional IRA

- **No deduction now:** contributions don't lower this year's taxes.
- **Tax-free later:** qualified withdrawals, including all the growth, are tax-free.
- **No required withdrawals** during the original owner's lifetime.

## Contribution limits and income limits

For 2026, the IRA contribution limit is $7,500 ($8,600 if you're 50 or older), shared with any traditional IRA. Your ability to contribute phases out at higher incomes:

- **Single:** $153,000 to $168,000
- **Married filing jointly:** $242,000 to $252,000

## Getting money out

- Your **contributions** can be withdrawn at any time without tax or penalty.
- **Earnings** are tax-free and penalty-free once you're 59½ and the account has been open at least five years. Earlier withdrawals of earnings may face tax and a 10% additional tax unless an exception applies.

## Who often likes a Roth

People early in their careers, who may be in a lower tax bracket now than they will be in retirement.
