# Target Date Funds

A target date fund is a single fund that holds a mix of stocks and bonds and automatically gets more conservative as a chosen year approaches, usually your expected retirement year.

## How they work

You pick the fund with the year closest to when you plan to retire, like a "2060" fund. Early on, it holds mostly stocks for growth. Over time, it gradually shifts toward bonds and cash to reduce risk. This planned shift is called the **glide path**.

## Why people use them

- **One-decision investing:** the fund handles diversification and rebalancing for you.
- **Common in 401(k)s:** often the default choice when you join a plan.

## Things to check

- **Not all funds with the same year are alike.** Different companies take different amounts of risk.
- **They don't stop at the target year.** Many keep adjusting for years after.
- **Fees vary.** Compare expense ratios.
- **No guarantee.** You can still lose money, including near or after the target date.
