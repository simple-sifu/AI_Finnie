# What Is a Bond?

A bond is a loan you make to a government or company. In return, they promise to pay you interest and give your money back on a set date.

## Key terms

- **Face value (par):** the amount you get back when the bond matures, often $1,000.
- **Coupon:** the interest rate the bond pays, usually twice a year.
- **Maturity:** the date the loan ends and you get your face value back.

**Example:** a $1,000 bond with a 4% coupon and a 10-year maturity pays $40 a year for 10 years, then returns your $1,000.

## Who issues bonds

- **The U.S. government** (Treasury bills, notes and bonds)
- **States and cities** (municipal bonds)
- **Companies** (corporate bonds)

## Why own bonds

Bonds usually move up and down less than stocks and provide steady income. Many investors hold bonds to balance the ups and downs of stocks.

## Risks

Bonds aren't risk-free. The issuer could fail to pay (credit risk), and bond prices fall when interest rates rise (interest rate risk).
