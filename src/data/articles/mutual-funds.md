# What Is a Mutual Fund?

A mutual fund pools money from many investors to buy a collection of stocks, bonds or other investments. When you buy shares of the fund, you own a slice of everything it holds.

## Why people use them

- **Instant diversification:** one fund can hold hundreds of companies.
- **Professional management:** someone else picks and manages the investments.
- **Small minimums:** many funds let you start with modest amounts.

## How pricing works

Mutual funds trade once a day, after the market closes, at a price called the **net asset value (NAV)**. Whether you place your order at 10 a.m. or 3 p.m., you get that day's closing NAV.

## Costs

Mutual funds charge yearly fees (the expense ratio), and some also charge sales loads or 12b-1 fees. Costs vary widely, so compare before you buy.

## Active and index funds

Some mutual funds are actively managed, trying to beat the market. Others are index funds that simply track a market index at a lower cost.
