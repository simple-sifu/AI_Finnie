# Market Indexes

A market index tracks the performance of a group of investments, giving a quick snapshot of how part of the market is doing.

## Well-known U.S. stock indexes

- **S&P 500:** about 500 large U.S. companies; the most common measure of "the market."
- **Dow Jones Industrial Average:** 30 large, well-known U.S. companies.
- **Nasdaq Composite:** thousands of companies listed on the Nasdaq exchange, with a heavy share of technology companies.

## Why indexes matter

- **News shorthand:** "The market was up 1% today" usually refers to an index like the S&P 500.
- **Benchmarks:** fund performance is often compared with an index.
- **Investing:** index funds and ETFs let you buy the whole index in one investment.

## You can't buy an index directly

An index is just a measurement. To invest in it, you buy a fund that tracks it, like an S&P 500 index fund. Funds that track the same index can still differ in fees.
