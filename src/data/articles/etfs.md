# What Is an ETF?

An exchange-traded fund (ETF) is a fund that holds a basket of investments, like a mutual fund, but trades on a stock exchange like a single stock.

## How ETFs work

- You buy and sell ETF shares through a brokerage account during the trading day.
- The price changes throughout the day as people trade.
- Many ETFs track an index, like the S&P 500 or the total U.S. stock market.

## Why beginners like them

- **Diversification:** one ETF can hold hundreds or thousands of companies or bonds.
- **Low costs:** many index ETFs have very low expense ratios.
- **Low minimums:** you can often buy a single share, and some brokers allow fractions of a share.

## Things to watch

- **Trading costs:** many brokers charge no commission, but there's still a small gap between the buying and selling price (the spread).
- **Not all ETFs are simple.** Some use leverage or complex strategies and are meant for short-term traders, not beginners.
