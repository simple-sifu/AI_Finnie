# Bond Yield

Yield is the return you get from an investment's income, shown as a percentage. For bonds, it tells you how much you're earning in interest.

## Coupon rate vs. yield

- **Coupon rate:** the fixed interest rate set when the bond is issued. A $1,000 bond with a 5% coupon pays $50 a year.
- **Current yield:** the yearly interest divided by the bond's current price. If that bond's price drops to $900, the current yield is about 5.6% ($50 ÷ $900).

## Yield to maturity

Yield to maturity estimates your total return if you hold the bond until it matures, counting interest payments plus any gain or loss from the price you paid. It's the most complete single measure.

## Why yield moves opposite to price

When a bond's price falls, its yield rises, because you're getting the same interest for less money. When its price rises, its yield falls.

## Keep in mind

A higher yield usually means higher risk. Before chasing the highest yield, check the bond's credit rating and maturity.
