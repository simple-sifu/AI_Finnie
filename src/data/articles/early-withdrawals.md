# Early Withdrawals from Retirement Accounts

Retirement accounts are meant for retirement. Taking money out early usually costs you.

## The general rule

If you withdraw from a 401(k) or IRA before age **59½**, you usually owe:

- regular income tax on the taxable amount, **plus**
- a **10% additional tax**.

**Example:** withdrawing $10,000 early could cost $1,000 in additional tax, on top of income tax.

## Common exceptions

The 10% additional tax may not apply in situations such as:

- total and permanent disability,
- certain unreimbursed medical expenses,
- leaving your job in or after the year you turn 55 (for workplace plans),
- up to $10,000 for a first-time home purchase (IRAs),
- qualified higher-education expenses (IRAs),
- a birth or adoption (up to $5,000),
- one emergency personal expense per year (up to $1,000).

Income tax may still apply even when the 10% additional tax doesn't. Rules differ between IRAs and workplace plans.

## Roth IRA contributions

You can withdraw your own Roth IRA **contributions** (not earnings) at any time without tax or penalty.

## Before withdrawing

Consider other options first, like an emergency fund. Money taken out early also loses its future growth.
