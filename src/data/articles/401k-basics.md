# 401(k) Plans

A 401(k) is a retirement savings plan offered by many employers. You choose how much of each paycheck to contribute, and the money is invested for retirement.

## Tax benefits

- **Traditional 401(k):** contributions come out of your paycheck before income tax, lowering your taxable income now. You pay tax when you withdraw in retirement.
- **Roth 401(k)** (if your plan offers it): contributions are made after tax, and qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free.

Either way, your investments grow without yearly taxes while they stay in the account.

## Employer match

Many employers add money when you contribute, such as 50 cents for every dollar you put in, up to a limit. This is often called "free money," and getting the full match is a common first goal.

## Rules to know

- There's a yearly limit on how much you can contribute (see "2026 Contribution Limits").
- Withdrawals before age 59½ usually face a 10% additional tax, plus income tax, unless an exception applies.
- For traditional 401(k)s, required minimum distributions generally start at age 73.

## Investment choices

Your plan offers a menu of funds. Target date funds are a common default.
