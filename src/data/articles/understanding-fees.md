# Understanding Investment Fees

Fees are what you pay to buy, hold or get advice on investments. They may look small, but they come out of your returns every year, and that adds up.

## Common fees

- **Expense ratio:** a yearly fee charged by mutual funds and ETFs, shown as a percentage of your investment.
- **Sales load:** a commission some mutual funds charge when you buy or sell.
- **Trading commissions:** a fee per trade. Many brokers now charge $0 for stock and ETF trades.
- **Advisory fees:** what you pay an adviser, often about 1% of your account per year.
- **Account fees:** annual or maintenance fees on some accounts.

## Why small fees matter

Say you invest $10,000 for 30 years at 7% a year:

- With 0.1% in yearly fees: about **$74,000**
- With 1% in yearly fees: about **$57,000**

That 0.9% difference costs about $17,000.

## What to do

Always ask what you'll pay, in dollars and percentages, before you invest. Compare funds' expense ratios. Lower-cost index funds are a popular choice for this reason.
