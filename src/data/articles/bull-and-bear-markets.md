# Bull and Bear Markets

"Bull" and "bear" describe the market's overall direction.

## Bull market

A period when prices are rising and investors are generally optimistic. It's commonly defined as a rise of 20% or more from a recent low.

## Bear market

A period when prices are falling and investors are pessimistic. It's commonly defined as a drop of 20% or more from a recent high.

## A smaller dip: correction

A drop of about 10% from a recent high is often called a **correction**. Corrections are more common than bear markets.

## What this means for you

- Bull and bear markets are both normal parts of the market cycle.
- Nobody can reliably predict when one will start or end.
- Long-term investors usually do best by sticking to their plan through both, rather than trying to jump in and out.

## Where the names come from

A common explanation: a bull attacks by thrusting its horns up, and a bear swipes its paws down.
