# Dollar-Cost Averaging

Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount of money on a regular schedule, such as $200 every month, no matter what the market is doing.

## How it works

When prices are high, your $200 buys fewer shares. When prices are low, it buys more. Over time, this can lower your average cost per share compared with trying to time a single big purchase.

| Month | Price per share | Shares bought with $200 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $20 | 10 |
| 2 | $16 | 12.5 |
| 3 | $25 | 8 |

## Why beginners like it

- You don't have to guess the "right time" to invest.
- It builds a steady habit.
- It takes emotion out of the decision.

If you contribute to a 401(k) from every paycheck, you're already dollar-cost averaging.

## Keep in mind

It doesn't guarantee a profit or protect against losses in a falling market. It's a way to invest steadily, not a way to avoid risk.
