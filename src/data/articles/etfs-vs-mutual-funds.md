# ETFs vs. Mutual Funds

ETFs and mutual funds both let you own many investments at once. The main differences are in how they trade, what they cost and how they're taxed.

| | ETFs | Mutual funds |
|---|---|---|
| How you trade | Throughout the day, like a stock | Once a day, at the closing price (NAV) |
| Minimum to start | Price of one share, or less with fractional shares | Often a set minimum, like $1,000 |
| Typical costs | Often low expense ratios | Ranges from very low (index funds) to high (some active funds) |
| Automatic investing | Depends on the broker | Easy to set up fixed-dollar purchases |
| Taxes in a taxable account | Usually more tax-efficient | May pass along more capital gains |

## Which should you choose?

For most beginners, the type of fund matters less than what it holds and what it costs. A low-cost index ETF and a low-cost index mutual fund that track the same index will behave very similarly.

## A quick guide

- Want to invest a fixed dollar amount automatically? Mutual funds can be easier.
- Investing in a taxable account? ETFs often have a tax edge.
- Either way, compare expense ratios first.
