# Traditional vs. Roth

Both traditional and Roth accounts (IRAs and 401(k)s) help you save for retirement with tax advantages. The difference is **when** you pay tax.

| | Traditional | Roth |
|---|---|---|
| Tax on contributions | Usually tax-deductible or pre-tax now | Paid now (no deduction) |
| Tax on growth | None while invested | None while invested |
| Tax on withdrawals | Taxed as income | Tax-free if qualified |
| Required minimum distributions | Yes, generally from age 73 | None for Roth IRAs or Roth 401(k)s during the owner's life |

## The key question

**Do you expect your tax rate to be higher now or in retirement?**

- If you think it's **lower now** (for example, early in your career), Roth often makes sense: pay tax at today's lower rate.
- If you think it's **higher now** (for example, at peak earnings), traditional often makes sense: take the deduction now.

## Not sure?

Nobody knows future tax rates. Many people split their savings between both types to spread out the risk. Having both can also give you flexibility in retirement.
