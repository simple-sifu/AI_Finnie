# Traditional IRAs

A traditional IRA (individual retirement arrangement) is a retirement account you open yourself, separate from any employer plan.

## Tax benefits

- Contributions may be **tax-deductible**, lowering your taxable income for the year.
- Investments grow without yearly taxes.
- Withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.

## Who can contribute

You need earned income (like wages) to contribute. For 2026, you can contribute up to $7,500, or $8,600 if you're 50 or older. That limit is shared across all your traditional and Roth IRAs.

## When the deduction is limited

If you or your spouse are covered by a workplace retirement plan, your deduction may be reduced or eliminated at higher incomes. For 2026, a single filer covered by a workplace plan sees the deduction phase out between $81,000 and $91,000 of income.

## Withdrawal rules

- Withdrawals before age 59½ generally face a 10% additional tax, plus income tax, unless an exception applies.
- Required minimum distributions generally begin at age 73.
