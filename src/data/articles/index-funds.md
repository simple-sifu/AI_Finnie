# Index Funds

An index fund is a mutual fund or ETF that tries to match the performance of a market index, like the S&P 500, instead of trying to beat it.

## How they work

The fund simply holds the same investments as the index, in roughly the same proportions. If the index goes up 8%, the fund aims to go up about 8%, minus its small fees.

## Why they're popular with beginners

- **Low costs:** with no team picking stocks, expense ratios are often very low.
- **Broad diversification:** a total-market index fund can hold thousands of companies.
- **Simplicity:** you don't need to pick winners.
- **Track record:** over long periods, many actively managed funds have trailed their index after fees.

## Common examples of what they track

- **S&P 500:** about 500 large U.S. companies.
- **Total U.S. stock market:** large, mid and small U.S. companies.
- **Total bond market:** a broad mix of U.S. investment-grade bonds.

## Keep in mind

An index fund matches the market, both up and down. If the market falls 20%, your index fund will fall about 20% too.
