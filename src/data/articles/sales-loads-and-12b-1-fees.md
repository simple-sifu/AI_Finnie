# Sales Loads and 12b-1 Fees

Some mutual funds charge fees beyond the expense ratio. Two common ones are sales loads and 12b-1 fees.

## Sales loads

A sales load is a commission paid when you buy or sell certain mutual funds, usually to compensate the broker who sold them.

- **Front-end load:** taken when you buy. With a 5% front-end load, only $9,500 of a $10,000 investment actually gets invested.
- **Back-end load:** charged when you sell, often shrinking the longer you hold.

## 12b-1 fees

12b-1 fees are ongoing yearly fees used to pay for marketing and distribution of the fund. They're included in the fund's expense ratio.

## How to avoid them

Many funds are **no-load**, meaning they charge no sales load. Many index funds and ETFs have no loads and low or no 12b-1 fees. Check a fund's prospectus or fee table before you buy.

## Why it matters

These fees come straight out of your returns. Paying a load means you start out behind before your investment has had a chance to grow.
